Eyes

Her most important feature. The two eyes never match: one broken TV screen, one purple.

Broken TV eye

Her right · under the bangs
In-world, glowing
Clean reference drawing
A dead TV screen instead of an iris: glossy near-black glass with faint cracks and scattered static pixels. A bright glitch band runs diagonally across it, hot pink with a white core and a thin green edge, plus a white glare on the far side. In low light it glows.

Purple eye

Her left · always visible
On the model
Big, glossy lilac-purple iris with a dark pupil and white highlight, thick black upper lash line. The eye sits inside a tan fur patch, and Borg-style copper implants (think Seven of Nine) are set into the brow above it.
In the dark: the TV eye is the one thing that lights up.
Artist take: painted with a rainbow-static screen. Colorful static is fine.
Artist notes
The TV eye is on the side her bangs fall over, so it often peeks out from under the hair.
No iris or pupil on the TV eye. Show it as a screen: flat dark glass, glitch band, static.
Don’t swap sides. The tan patch and copper implants always go with the purple eye; the scar goes with the TV eye.
Some 3D renders show an older starry-sparkle version of this eye. Draw the broken-TV version instead. However, I love seeing artists having fun with this and doing it how they like within the glitchy style.

Scar & implants

Scars and cybernetics that are easy to miss. The scars sit with the TV eye; the implants with the purple eye.

Face scars

TV-eye side
Two parallel slash scars cut diagonally through the TV eye: they start on the brow above it and carry on down the cheek toward the muzzle. Grey-brown with cross-hatched stitch marks along their length; the upper cut has a pinker, fresher-looking healed line.

Copper implants

Purple-eye side
Borg-style cybernetic implants, like Seven of Nine’s: flat copper plates and short metal ridges set into the brow above the purple eye, with a copper ring around the eye itself. A thin copper band runs from the brow implants up and back, curving down behind the ear, with two round glowing yellow lights where it sits above the ear. Metallic, not hair.

Body scars

Torso
Several short, raised pink scar lines scattered across her torso: under the chest, across the belly and on both sides of the waist.
Mostly horizontal with a few on an angle, each about a finger or two long, slightly puffy with darker edges. They sit on top of the fur pattern, spots and all.

Head & hair

Hair

Pale butter-blonde, shoulder length. A heavy side-swept fringe falls across her right eye (the TV eye); the back is gathered into a long, loose ponytail that falls past the shoulders.

Ears

Large, floppy, held out sideways. Tan outer rim, textured dusty-pink inside.

Muzzle

Wide, rounded dusty-pink nose pad with a fine bumpy texture and two curved nostrils. Cream chin, gentle closed smile.

Power wheelchair

Part of her everyday design. She sits relaxed and confident in it, often leaning on an armrest.
Joystick
Controller box with a round-knob joystick at the end of her right armrest, cable looping down to the base.
Wheels
Big mid drive wheels with red rim rings, plus small casters front and back: six wheels in total.

Build

Frame: matte black, sleek and modern, with red accent stripes on the seat sides, base and wheel rims.
Seat: contoured black captain-style seat and backrest, adjustable headrest, padded flip-up armrests.
Feet: one wide footplate at the front where her hooves rest.
Real-world reference

The actual chair

Her chair is based on this real Quickie mid-wheel-drive power chair. Use it for shapes and details: the headrest bracket, the curved seat sides, the joystick arm, the six-wheel base and the footplate.
The real chair’s accents are orange-red; in her art they’re a slightly deeper red. Either is fine.
